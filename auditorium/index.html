UNESCO's mission

Protecting cultural heritage and promoting creativity

UNESCO is the UN agency promoting cooperation in education, science, culture and communication to foster peace worldwide. It assists Member States by setting global norms and standards, building cooperation tools, generating policy knowledge, and creating worldwide site networks.

Its work with 194 Member States includes protecting biodiversity, addressing AI challenges, ensuring quality education, and safeguarding cultural heritage.

The fight against illicit trafficking of cultural property

The 1970 Convention

A landmark treaty adopted on 14 November 1970 — the first binding legal instrument to recognize the need for coordinated international action to combat cultural property trafficking.

Learn more about the 1970 Convention

Combatting illicit trafficking of cultural property: UNESCO's role

Cultural objects that have survived centuries form part of our shared identity. Theft and trafficking deprive communities of memory and cultural rights, fuel organized crime, money laundering and sometimes terrorism financing — affecting origin, transit and destination countries alike. Preserving heritage is a UNESCO priority, as the only United Nations agency specifically mandated for culture.

UNESCO's role in the return and restitution of cultural property

Restitution has long been central to UNESCO's mission. The Organization serves as a multilateral platform for cooperation, dialogue, and support to Member States — an issue that touches identity, spirituality, education, justice and development, particularly through museums.
