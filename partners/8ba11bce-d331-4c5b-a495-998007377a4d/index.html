About the UNESCO Virtual Museum of Stolen Cultural Objects

A project to raise awareness about the illicit trafficking of cultural property

The project was announced by UNESCO's Director-General at the MONDIACULT 2022 Conference in Mexico. It responds to Member States' calls for coordinated international action to raise public awareness, especially among youth, and aligns with UNESCO's mission of peace, intercultural dialogue, heritage protection, and UN resolutions.

Illicit trafficking deprives peoples of their history and identity, and represents a global threat

UNESCO relies on its Conventions and Recommendations, notably the 1970 Convention on prohibiting illicit import, export and transfer of cultural property, plus the 2015 Recommendation on Museums and Collections and the 2003 Convention for Intangible Cultural Heritage — since tangible objects carry practices, knowledge and memories.

An ambitious project harnessing digital technologies

Launched in 2022 with support from Saudi Arabia, later joined by the USA and Greece, the digital museum features objects digitised in 2D or 3D, enriched with community narratives and testimonies to restore context, offering an immersive experience focused on younger generations.

Francis Kéré's vision

In 2023, Pritzker Prize-winning architect Francis Kéré was entrusted with the visual design. His concept draws on the baobab tree, a symbol of resilience, treating heritage as communities' 'roots' and linking tangible and intangible heritage — both harmed by trafficking.

2024: Member States invited to submit stolen and missing objects

Member States submitted files of objects listed in INTERPOL's Stolen Works of Art database along with educational content. They also helped create the 'Return and Restitution Room', showcasing restituted objects and good practices — a space of hope. The museum becomes a living, evolving, immersive educational platform where communities give objects a voice.

An ever-growing museum

Launched in September 2025 at MONDIACULT 2025 in Barcelona — described as the project's "true beginning". The Gallery of Stolen Objects is meant to shrink as objects are recovered, while the Return and Restitution Room grows. Aimed at youth and digital natives, the museum seeks to be a unifying platform that turns visitors into "agents of change" protecting shared heritage.
