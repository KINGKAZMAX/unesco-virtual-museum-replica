Fight illicit trafficking

Combatting Illicit Trafficking of Cultural Property: UNESCO's role

Cultural property has sometimes crossed centuries before reaching us

Items that survive across centuries serve as witnesses to shared history and identity, yet theft, looting, and trafficking rob communities of memory and cultural rights. These acts fuel organized crime, money laundering, and sometimes terrorism financing, affecting origin, transit and destination countries alike. UNESCO — the only UN agency with a culture-specific mandate — prioritizes heritage preservation, drawing on instruments such as the 1970 Convention on prohibiting illicit import, export and transfer of cultural property.

A holistic strategy built around three pillars

  • Preventive measures: strengthening museum security, inventories, export certificates, awareness campaigns, art-market due diligence, and training for law enforcement, heritage professionals, the judiciary, and art-market actors.
  • Return and restitution: a top priority under and beyond the 1970 Convention; UNESCO supports Member States, facilitates dialogue, and promotes new cooperation and agreements.
  • International cooperation: building national and regional mechanisms with partners (INTERPOL, WCO, UNIDROIT, UNODC, MERCOSUR, AUC, EU, ICA, ICOM) to harmonize legal frameworks, strengthen capacities, and advocate in multilateral forums.

UNESCO has developed resources including the Database of National Cultural Heritage Laws, the UNESCO–WCO Model Export Certificate, the International Code of Ethics for Dealers in Cultural Property, and Model Provisions on preventing illicit trafficking.

Culture in emergencies

In conflict or disaster, heritage is especially vulnerable due to its fragility and symbolic value, yet culture also supports community recovery and resilience. UNESCO protects movable cultural property during emergencies, conflicts, and natural hazards, implementing its mandate in line with Security Council resolutions and the Pact for the Future adopted at the UN Summit of the Future in September 2024.
