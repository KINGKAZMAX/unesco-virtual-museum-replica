INTERPOL

Founded in 1923, INTERPOL is the world's largest international police organization, with 196 member countries working together to address transnational crime, including the trafficking of cultural property. Its collaboration with UNESCO is presented as a model of cultural and security institutions joining forces to protect humanity's shared heritage.

Report information

If you have information about a stolen object from the Virtual Museum, you can contact INTERPOL via a contact form. Report missing and stolen heritage items directly to INTERPOL to help global law enforcement track, recover, and return cultural property.

Learn more about INTERPOL's activities

The Stolen Works of Art Database is a central pillar of INTERPOL's mission against cultural heritage crime — mainly accessible to authorized law enforcement and cultural professionals, holding records of thousands of stolen and missing artworks. It serves police, customs officers, art dealers, journalists, universities and museums with real-time checks on suspicious objects.

INTERPOL also launched the ID-Art mobile App, a public version of the database, letting police, customs officials, the public, collectors, dealers, journalists, students and enthusiasts help identify stolen cultural property. The organization publishes Purple Notices to share information on criminals' methods of operation and concealment.

International operations

INTERPOL organizes international operations and investigations targeting trafficking networks. Operations such as Pandora, Athena, and Synergia unite police and customs agencies across continents to identify smuggling routes, seize illicit objects, and dismantle criminal networks. These efforts show cultural property crime is a lucrative global industry often tied to organized crime.

Training and capacity-building are central, with workshops and materials for police, customs agents, prosecutors, and museum staff, so frontline responders can detect and respond to heritage crime.

INTERPOL works closely with UNESCO, the World Customs Organization (WCO), ICOM, and other cultural organizations, underscoring the global nature of the challenge and the need for coordinated responses.
