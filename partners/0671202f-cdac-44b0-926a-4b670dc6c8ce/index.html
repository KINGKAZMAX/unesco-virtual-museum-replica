UNESCO's mission

UNESCO's Mission: Protecting Cultural Heritage

Respect and human dignity require the acceptance of beliefs, diversity and the preservation of traditions

These are all areas defended and promoted by UNESCO, which is the only institution to make a correlation between the right to culture and development. Culture has a fundamental role in our societies. Through culture people can discover their common humanity and become free and enlightened citizens. It is both the backbone through which we form society, as well as an important driver of transformation, cohesion and development. Culture shapes identities, fuels creativity and develops innovative responses to contemporary challenges. Culture connects us to our past and guides our future, underpinning social, economic and environmental well-being.

The MONDIACULT 2022 World Conference unanimously adopted a Ministerial Declaration

For the first time, it recognises culture as a "global public good", calling for culture to become an independent goal in future development frameworks. Cultural rights, as defined by this Declaration, span a wide range of cultural expressions — from tangible and intangible heritage to creative industries, including in the digital environment. They are essential for building just, inclusive and equitable societies, enabling all individuals — especially marginalised and vulnerable groups — to fully engage with culture, benefit from it and reflect their diverse points of view and practices in development efforts. In a context of growing global challenges such as conflicts, epidemics, climate change and technological progress, UNESCO emphasises the need to preserve culture for both individuals and societies, highlighting culture as a driver of recovery, strengthening communities' resilience.

As the sole UN agency with a specific mandate for cultural heritage protection, UNESCO's cultural action focuses on four key challenges:

  • Protect culture in crisis situations — in situations of armed conflict or disaster, culture is particularly threatened due to its inherent vulnerability and its high symbolic value;
  • Adapt to contemporary challenges such as sustainable development, climate change and biodiversity conservation;
  • Preserve heritage through the protection of cultural and natural heritage, underwater cultural heritage, the preservation of diversity through intangible cultural heritage, and the prevention and combating of illicit trafficking of cultural property;
  • Build sustainable, inclusive and creative societies by promoting creativity, supporting artistic innovation and recognising the importance of livelihoods in the creative economy.

Museums lie at the heart of UNESCO's mission

They play a crucial role in promoting cultural diversity, fostering global networks, facilitating intersectoral and cross-border cooperation, and empowering Member States to safeguard arts, culture, heritage and creativity for present and future generations. Since its inception, UNESCO has supported the development of museums worldwide by establishing norms, guiding policies, implementing projects and providing training. Their remarkable diversity is intrinsically linked to the Organization's programmes and cultural conventions. By implementing its cultural conventions and recommendations — which reinforce one another — UNESCO works with the international community to protect cultural heritage and promote the diversity of cultural expressions. As the United Nations' specialised agency for heritage protection and the promotion of creativity, UNESCO carries out its mandate in alignment with relevant UN Security Council resolutions.
