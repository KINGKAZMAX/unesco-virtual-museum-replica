Togo Case Study: Strengthening Cultural Heritage Protection

Beyond the online experience, the Virtual Museum has an impact on the ground: it supports Member States in protecting cultural heritage and effectively combating illicit trafficking.

The pilot project carried out in Togo in early 2025

The UNESCO Virtual Museum of Stolen Cultural Property is not just a digital experience; above all, it is a project that aims to have a real impact on the ground — to recover stolen cultural property and support all Member States in protecting their cultural heritage. To ensure that all Member States can benefit from it, the Virtual Museum project also includes capacity-building activities, tailored to specific needs, to support States that do not yet have national inventories, inventories of stolen objects, or objects registered in the INTERPOL Stolen Works of Art Database.

The pilot project carried out in Togo in early 2025 fully illustrates this potential. Co-funded by the Virtual Museum project, it enabled ten Togolese heritage professionals to be trained in international inventory standards, in cooperation with ICOM Africa and under the direction of Jean-Paul Koudougou — in particular in the use of the Object ID standard and the ID-Art mobile application. At the same time, a multi-stakeholder consultation led by Vincent Négri, an expert in cultural heritage law, brought together 26 actors from the cultural, legal, environmental, and research sectors. This led to the creation of a committee tasked with drafting a preliminary bill and implementing regulations.

The project led to the establishment of an inventory of fifty masterpieces from five Togolese national museums and the drafting of concrete proposals to strengthen the national legal framework for the prevention of illicit trafficking.

Prevention in action

This activity in Togo demonstrates how essential preventive measures are to fight the illicit trafficking of cultural property. Through concrete actions on the ground, UNESCO supports Member States in implementing sustainable preventive measures, such as documenting, securing, and promoting their collections. By combining digital innovation and capacity building on the ground, the Virtual Museum promotes the preservation and protection of cultural property within a collective and shared dynamic, serving the cultural heritage and identity of communities.
